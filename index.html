How the physics works

The ball is a rigid sphere with linear velocity and a 3D spin vector. At the contact patch the lane applies Coulomb friction opposite to the slip velocity (v + w x r). On oil the friction coefficient is ~0.04, so the ball skids and barely changes direction. Past the end of the pattern the friction jumps to ~0.22: the side-roll component of the spin is now dragged by the lane, which pushes the ball sideways (the hook) while it also converts spin into forward roll. Once the slip velocity reaches zero the ball rolls, the hook stops, and it travels straight into the pins (this is why the breakpoint matters). Pins are 3.5 lb bodies that topple when struck faster than a threshold, then slide and knock into neighbours and kickbacks. The strike % button adds realistic human error (about half a board at the arrows, a quarter mph, 15 rpm) to show how forgiving your line really is.
